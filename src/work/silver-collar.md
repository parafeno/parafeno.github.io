In his 1951 book *White Collar*, C. Wright Mills gave a sociological name to the class anxiety of an entire generation: a critical portrait of the new American middle class. White collar, blue collar: a taxonomy that was never meant to include the artist, the one who works with the whole of themselves.

The total liberalisation of financial markets, together with the democratisation of media, has turned the whole world into a casino. The glossy varnish of fame and recognition feels ever more within reach, and that promise of escape holds the most power over those who were stripped of their humanity from the very start.

The piece resurrects El Pozí, a queer personality from Cádiz who rose to sudden fame in the 2000s, first through a series of interviews on Jesús Quintero's show and later on *Crónicas Marcianas*. Set on top of the much-talked-about Nvidia, OpenAI, Oracle and Microsoft investment bubble, the piece tells a fable about stardom and speculation: from the most absolute margin to overnight celebrity, and finally back to destitution when his manager stole everything the industry had let him earn for a moment.

The 3D bust, subtly animated, plays in a continuous loop, a fixed performance that runs its course regardless of who stands in front of it or how many. At times it looks away for a moment, as if in a fit of shame, only to return its gaze to the viewer just as piercingly. His story traces the full arc: extraction, abandonment, the spectacle of ruin. The bubble, and the burst.

I grew up in Barbate, the same town in Cádiz where he was born. As a dissident artist, his trajectory resonates beyond the anecdotal: difference turned into spectacle, the hunger for recognition that systems exploit and discard, the intermediaries who extract and vanish.

The piece is, among other things, an act of remembrance for someone my town created and forgot. And a reflection on the intermediaries, agents, managers, curators and platforms that mediate and extract value from other people's images, and on what happens to those squeezed by a system that never had a name for them in the first place.
